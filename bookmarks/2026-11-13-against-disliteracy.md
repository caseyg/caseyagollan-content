Mandy Brown:

> When we talk about an absence of literacy, about how few people seem able to read thoughtfully or deeply, we are not talking about something that merely happens, an occurrence without actor or reason, like an asteroid slicing through the atmosphere. We are witness to a strategic all-out attack and the ruins it leaves behind. We are not post-literate but dis-literate, subject to a concerted and well-funded effort to separate us from literacy, an effort proliferated across schools, workplaces, and, perhaps most perniciously, our homes and pockets.
> From channel surfing to doomscrolling, we have long been inundated with technology designed to stupefy and placate. The latest assault tries to transform reading from relation into information. Rather than a collaboration between reader and writer, we get knowledge stripped of its context and color, reduced to data points presented in such a way as to brook no dissent. Let the machines read for us while we lay about, listening to neat summaries of books and papers, smug in our time-saving abilities—though what, precisely, we have saved that time for remains to be seen. Let the machines write for us too, displacing thinking and making with recording, every answer to every possible question available at the barest command. No curiosity, no desire that cannot be immediately sated and therefore squashed before it comes to life.
